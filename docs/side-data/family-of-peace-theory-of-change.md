# Side data — the originating organisation's theory of change ("Family of Peace")

> **Side data, not part of the paper.** This page transcribes, in public-health logic-model form,
> the theory of change that the originating organisation uses for its own programmes. It is
> provided so a reader can see the organisational logic within which the T-PHE synthesis was made.
> T-PHE does **not** adopt this theory of change: T-PHE's failure criterion excludes marriage
> continuation and divorce reduction as success measures, whereas the organisation's theory of
> change lists them as outcomes. All content is *organisation-attested* (internal record dated
> 13 July 2025; effective in governance 21 June 2026); achieved figures are *not independently
> assured*. No statement here is evidence of effectiveness.

## 1. Problem statement (as framed by the organisation)

Families are vulnerable to personal and structural conflict; domestic violence and exploitation
of women and children; marriages entered in bad faith; working-age men lacking family-leadership
and management skills; deep cultural differences across and within religions; and the absence of
a safe digital support system for families.

## 2. Intended impact and Sustainable Development Goal mapping

| Working-age Muslim men develop responsible family leadership and household economic management | 8 |
| The family becomes the origin of peace in a multicultural society; family institution more stable and resilient | 16 |
| A safe digital family network for digital citizenship | — |

## 3. Logic model

| Level | Content |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Team with long field experience; mediation and process expertise; technology application; a digital safe space (beta); monitoring stack; website and social channels; a nikah-guardian network; links to religious, private and academic bodies; social-enterprise status; office, training equipment, e-donation system |
| **Activities** | Group premarital training (culture, religion, values, finance, law, rights, duties); relationship-preserving weddings bridging two cultures; family dialogue sessions; content production on family strength and multiculturalism; a digital safe space for family support; cross-cultural family communication and mediation; micro-learning and reflection tools |
| **Outputs** | E-book, online course, self-assessment tools; cross-cultural families with access to the digital safe space; multimedia on rights, relationships and coexistence |
| **Short-term outcomes** | Couples and families aware of rights and duties; men develop family-leadership skills; cross-cultural families reduce conflict |
| **Medium-term outcomes** | Families more stable and resilient; digital safe-space network expands; violent divorce reduced |
| **Impact** | As in section 2 |

## 4. Stated assumptions (organisation's own, unverified in this repository)

1. Comprehensive premarital training with relational-ethics communication reduces divorce risk.
2. Weddings that preserve relationships between families build positive bonds and reduce cultural conflict.
3. Social support from surrounding people stabilises a marriage more than isolation.
4. A systematic, amicable divorce reduces harm to women and children.
5. Understanding-based management of cross-cultural families strengthens stability and peace.
6. A safe digital support system expands low-cost support networks.
7. Deep listening and positive communication reduce family conflict and bridge cultural difference.
8. Empowering families and communities to own the process produces grassroots systemic change.

The organisation cites relationship-education and family-studies literature in support of these
assumptions. Those citations were not verified in this repository and are not reproduced here.

## 5. Operating principles

Revenue with social impact under the Social Enterprise Act; positive peace, justice and compassion
in the family; a safe space to listen, speak and learn without judgement; empowerment so that
families support each other from the grassroots; cultural sensitivity toward religion, culture and
lifestyle; community-driven systemic transformation; digital readiness and safe technology use.

## 8. How this relates to T-PHE (and how it does not)

| Organisation's theory of change | T-PHE (the paper) |
|---|---|
| Success includes reduced divorce and more stable marriages | Marriage completion and continuation are **excluded** from the success criteria; a programme that raises them while leaving informed choice and accountable referral unchanged **fails** |
| Assumes premarital training reduces divorce risk | Makes no such assumption; proposes decision control as the mechanism, to be tested |
| Couple-level and family-level outcomes | Individual participant is the unit of protection; each person has a private channel |
| Activities include family mediation | Joint work stops where fear or coercive control appears; no couple mediation where coercive control is confirmed |
| Impact framed through SDGs and peacebuilding | Outcomes framed through public-health prevention: literacy, coercion recognition, safe help-seeking, referral fidelity, harms |

The two are compatible only in the sense that both start from the family as a safety environment.
T-PHE should be cited on its own terms, and the organisation's theory of change should not be
cited as evidence for T-PHE.
